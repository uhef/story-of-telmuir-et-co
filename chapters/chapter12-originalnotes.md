
Fragment from the diaries of Telmuir the Druid Dwarf

Today we finally wondered in to the mournlands. What a desolate, dead and unnatural place this is. I immediatelly felt unwell when we entered the mist. I truly hope that we find Uthrak swiftly and either deal with him or bring him to justice to answer for his deeds against the nature. Last night wasn't peaceful either. We could hear all sort of noises of battle and spell-casting from Gorgonhorn. Perhaps we shall learn later what the commotion was about.

As if mournlands wasn't bad enough, we lost Sylvanus yesterday. She was a trigger happy old drunk with zero self protection but through the years I had grown to like her a lot. Also we shared the same passion towards nature and now I feel very much alone in that regard. Some of my old circle of moon mentors have told me of powerful magic that could bring one back even from this stage but these powers are beyond me. In desperation I tried to fit her body to the bottomless magic bag we are carrying with us so that I could have brough her to the elderly in the druid circle in a hope of bringing her back. However she wouldn't fit through the narrow opening of the cursed bag and for some reason Phonzy insisted on burning her body so I had to drop that. Nothing would make me happier than to find a way to communicate with her spirit in my dreams. Nothing except a tiny pin was left of her ashes after the burning. Somehow this pin of bow and arrow survived the heat unaffected. I snatched it and attahced it to my shirt under the cloak. Somehow it feels even more easy than before to navigate in the nature. Perhaps parts of her ranger skills were merged within the artifact.

As said, the mournlands are making me sick. Everything is (un)dead here except for some randomn, wicked beasts that we have encountered. Nothing grows here and I haven't found anything natural since we entered the mist. Pretty soon after entering we heard sounds of a cat. Hopeful that we would actually find something living here, we approached the sound. I was already preparing myself to exchange a few words with the cat about the surrounding area but this was wishful thinking. Of course, this turned out to be some awful beast that just happened to make sounds like an ordinary house cat. A brief battle ensued. The strange thing is that this place seems to also affect the magic me and Phonzy are casting. It can affect the results in unpredictable ways. Luckily we also have members like Thule and Oliva that resort to physical power and dexterity when it comes to combat.

Wondering further into the fog we found bodies of all sort of creatures: orcs, goblins and dwarves too. Some had died and frozen in mid-movement. Perhaps I could plant some Panya seeds I have with me somewhere here. Perhaps with some magical protection I could make them resist the fog an grow just a bit so that I could create a small isle of comfort in this otherwise dead place.

As we pushed deeper into the mist-shrouded ruins, we stumbled upon a strange sight—some kind of metallic drum, dented and worn, with an equally metallic being hammering away at it. It was shaped like a humanoid, though clearly artificial, and surprisingly expressive for something forged. Oliva took it upon herself to make contact, and to our amazement, the construct answered her questions. It called itself G.R.E.P—or at least that’s how I later came to spell it. At the time it pronounced the name with such mechanical staccato that we weren't entirely sure whether it was a name or a directive.

G.R.E.P. explained it was searching for artifacts and hinted that the place had once been an engineer’s forge. This sparked our interest, and when it seemed unable to open the drum—now clearly a sealed vault—we offered our help. The surface bore a numerical keypad, and we remembered an old note we'd found with a cryptic string of numbers. Punching in the sequence caused the vault to groan open, albeit halfway. It took a powerful blow from G.R.E.P.'s hammer to fully release the lock. The stale, metallic air that rushed out was somehow refreshing after the clinging mists of the Mournlands.

When we inquired about Uthrak, G.R.E.P. became evasive. Curious. It seemed to have no master, no orders—only a compulsion to collect, admire, and make sense of the arcane and mechanical. A lost soul made of steel, seeking purpose in ruins. It resonated with me more than I’d expected.

Beyond the vault was a cave, and signs of the old forge became clear. This was no simple blacksmith’s corner—it was the hidden workshop of House Cannith engineers. Every wall whispered of arcane industry. G.R.E.P. guided us, or perhaps just marched ahead, through a series of corridors until we came to a locked door. Beyond it, the rhythmic pounding of something massive.

Oliva tried one of the numeric codes, but it was wrong. The floor hissed and sparked with lightning. Phonzy cleverly noted the shock area was small, and using mage hand, we tried code after code until the door opened—revealing a far larger construct: a hulking, ancient warforged titan.

It demanded an access code we could not supply. We hesitated. Too long. The machine judged us hostile.

What followed was chaos. G.R.E.P. rushed in, only to be struck with a hammer that made the earth tremble. I cast lightning bolt—my magic crackled violently, disrupted again by this twisted realm, but it landed true. Phonzy shattered the silence with Shatter spells. The titan recoiled, but did not falter.

Then, with a roar of steel and stone, it broke through the wall to reach us.

Oliva, ever brave, launched a magical stone that struck friend and foe alike. I channeled healing into G.R.E.P., though the air itself seemed to drink the spell’s energy. Phonzy’s final blast reduced the titan to shrapnel—its limbs scattering like dried branches. A creature of the first generation, soulless and sleepless. Now, it sleeps forever.

Inside, the forge revealed its secrets. Cannith architecture—immaculate, maddening in its complexity. A full smelting and production line, levers and glyphs, and an incomplete sequence chart. From the design notes, we deduced the missing numbers. It became clear: this was where they created the earliest warforged—perhaps even colossi.

We showed G.R.E.P. the sigil of House Cannith. It hesitated, almost reverent. There was something more in its posture now. Phonzy speculated aloud: could G.R.E.P. have been rejected by the Lord of Blades? Was this one of Uthrak’s many faces?

We fed the forge three distinct arcane chunks—materials we’d salvaged earlier. A lever revealed a hidden Fibonacci sequence on the wall, which Oliva traced with her dagger. The forge roared to life, ancient magics awakening after centuries.

The result: a staff. Beautiful, strange. Bearing the mark of House Cannith, strung with delicate coils like bowstrings. I took it into my hands—it hummed with potential, not unlike the living wood of the Eldeen Reaches, but forged instead from logic and fire. G.R.E.P. announced something—“CCX 47: Protocol Initiate”—then stood quiet, contemplative.

I have no idea what we’ve unleashed.
