Fragment from the diaries of Telmuir the Druid Dwarf

Entry XXIII – The Ambush at Uthrak’s Den

The ashes of Jartsa still clung to our cloaks when we turned our thoughts to Uthrak. That despicable nature-hating fiend has cheated the grave too many times already, and I could feel the spirits stirring in the soil beneath my boots — the very earth and soil grows weary of his tread.

We needed cunning, not just steel. Or so we thought.
Our plan, like all good dwarven stonework, was carefully carved: G.R.E.P. would be our messenger, our lure. He’d speak of Jartsa’s fall and the map we’d taken — whisper of a rendezvous and betrayal. The hope was to draw Uthrak out, or at least thin his ranks.

But words, I’ve learned, are trickier tools than hammers.
And G.R.E.P., for all his gears and logic, speaks with more sparks than subtlety.

The guards saw through the tale faster than a goblin spots a coin purse. And so, before the plan had even drawn its first breath, it died a noisy death — replaced by the old, reliable language of violence.

What followed was chaos worthy of a saga.
A titan, a mountain of metal, stormed through the doors. Fire and lightning danced around us when Phonzy’s wall of flame turned the office front into a furnace. A well-placed lighting bolt added to the fiery storm we had created. The battle scene quickly turned into a hellfire with acrid smells of ashes and sulfur that seems to become a trademark of our party.

Once the titan had falled, Uthrak appeared from the side of the building. We turned our blows to him but each time we strike at that cursed wretch, he seems to draw strength from the very blows meant to fell him. He drank lightning as though it were mead, and when he shouted to Phonzy to join forces, I swear by the roots of the world-tree — something in the tone chilled my blood. What did he mean by that? And what fire burns behind Phonzy’s eyes that I’ve yet to see?

Oliva fought like a storm given shape.
Her staff cracked against Uthrak’s skull with the sound of justice, and Thule — clever fool — nearly fried himself to bring G.R.E.P. back from ruin, reattaching cables by instinct or miracle.

Uthrak turned to escape but being determined that we will not lose him this time, I summoned the nature to help me. My wolves leapt from the ether, brave as always, and started off a chase. 

After a brief hesitation, we charged after Uthrak and the wolf pack, the scent of burning stone thick in the air. Some distance down the building we could witness the whole pack of wolves slayed by Uthrak’s cruel hand. I’ll not forget that sight soon.

Up the stairs we ran, G.R.E.P. proclaiming, in his machine certainty, that Uthrak was “sixty-seven percent likely” to be above. I didn’t understand the measure, but it sounded sure enough to me.

Then — the room at the top. Empty.
A spiral stair that vanished into the ceiling like a riddle. Oliva vanished into it without hesitation, as though swallowed by the air itself. One by one, we followed, shouting oaths and battle cries into the unknown.

Only Phonzy lingered — greed’s shadow always close behind him. I caught a glimpse before I ascended — him staring at a glass case filled with trinkets of old magic. The fool grabbed one, a rod wreathed in flame patterns. It clung to him as though it had been waiting for that very moment.

Then came the roar — walls bursting, fire devouring air and flesh alike. I felt the heat chase him up the stairs, heard his scream, smelled scorched cloth and skin. But he made it. Barely.

And now… we rise.
Into flame, into smoke, into whatever waits above.
If Uthrak stands there, this time the earth will not grant him another escape.

By root and stone, I swear it.
